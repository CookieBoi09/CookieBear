Community > Program popularity

This is very true especially in high school when you will be trying to figure out which group of people you vibe with the most and will also be true beyond that time scale whether that’s in university years or in the workplace. If your school has programs or you know some outside of your school, you would want to make sure that you can envision yourself working in the respective community, because these people will be your “extended family” who you will likely work with for many years. You will have an exponentially better experience joining a lesser-known program full of people you enjoy working with (outside of having fun with) than a top program with people do may not vibe with or be able to work with as well. It’s true that everybody is different and having to adapt to different working cultures is an important skill in life, but you wouldn’t want to lose your individuality or your stance in your values in exchange for to be able to work with others.

Your trajectory for your GPA > Your current GPA

Just for some context, my high school uses powerschool, which shows your grades live on a week-by-week basis, meaning you can see where your grades are at any given week. To be frank, I am guilty of this all the time back in the day when “GPA seems to be everything”, but you ought to worry more about the habits and motivation behind your GPA than the actual grades. You can have a good grade in the first quarter and have a “surprising” bad grade in the next if the bad study habits are not taken care of.

As a side note, study habits aren’t limited to habits purely focused on studying. Habits relating to taking care of your health is equally as important if not more important than your study habits.(I will explain more of this later)

Sleep > studying

Having full 7-8 hours of sleep gives benefits such as a fully functional brain and a good high school experience. I understand for some people that this is very difficult especially for students who are taking heavy courseload during their Junior year (I was also in that same position, which was not pleasant unsurprisingly), but try to at least not have a bad sleep schedule. It does you more good to sleep more in the long run than sacrificing sleep for studying or homework. The other reason is that it’s easy to get caught in the spiral of doom from sleep deficiency, where lack fo sleep decreases your ability to do homework, so you spend more time on homework and therefore get less hours of sleep. This is what causes 99% of the stress in high school, so I beg you to avoid this fate because I don’t believe that high school students deserve to have this quantity of stress. This energy is better spent on exploring ideas and fields of study than stressing from lack of sleep.

It’s okay to not know what your doing, but deciding on one earlier is significantly better than later.

I didn’t know I would be doing physics in university when I first entered high school, but I knew that I wanted to do some form of STEM field such as computer science and mathematics. Growing up in silicon valley I was surrounded by the news of many technological advancements, especially from reading IEEE and Nature articles. This sparked my inspiration to pursue STEM, though I wasn’t hyper focused on a specific field, and that’s completely okay. I will say that being inspired by these things at a young age is what helped me with my sense of purpose and drive during high school years. As long as you have some form of aspirations of what you want to do in the future, you ought to hold on it tightly because the moments when you question your life choices is when you need this sense of purpose the most. There are many moments in my life when I wanted to give up studying school materials (due to exhaustion or “feeling empty”), but my curiosity in the natural phenomena of the world kept reminding me to push since I will be exploring them in university years if I put in the work during high school. You don’t have to have your entire life planned out, but having a general direction of what you want to do will set you up for success in whichever thing you decide to pursue in the future.
