&emsp;To be honest, I have no clue how difficult and frustrating it would be to be trying to apply for an internship for the summer of next year. From what I have heard, applying through the company websites is nearly impossible for getting an internship and meeting the recruiters irl is the best way to get an internship. Even though I have completed an internship during the summer before my senior year, it seems that even having one is not enough for the second.

&emsp;The even frustrating thing is that I have been “turned down” simply because I am a college freshman, which I have no control over in terms of the timing of where I am at now, without really looking into my resume. Don’t get me wrong, it’s not impossible to get an internship or even a job during freshman year, but it’s very difficult to have one secured unless you have referrals or connections.

&emsp;The thing about connections is that while on the surface level it seems unfair that some people who know someone in the inside can have a competitive edge over another person(And it really is from a competition view), tne reason some companies have referrals is because of how familiarity signals potential.

&emsp;Let’s say recruiter X is looking for some interns for next year and hears about a friends kid who is said to be very good a coding and has a good work ethic. X who’s currently looking at applications doesn’t really see the work ethic behind papers, but the qualifications look very good on paper. If you were to be in X’s position who’s looking over thousands of applications, would you try to interview a lot of people when you have someone you know and trust tells you that an applicant has great talent(as you have observed on paper and through a call) and ability to be hardworking? (Just think about it and you will have your answer)

&emsp;At the end of the day, whether I get an internship or not won’t determine the level of work I will do in the subsequent time because I can just continue to put in the work until it pays off in the end.
