As someone who is going to study physics in Mathematics and Physical Sciences Division in L&S(Letters and Sciences) in Berkeley, I will detail most of the the information I can about the general path I took to get to where I am now.(Most of these tips are specifically geared toward UC Berkeley requirements)

Note: The categories are work in progress and I will update them over time.

The reason I say this is because amist the competition between students, it's important to consider the idea that you are trying to built an identity which you will demonstrate to the universities. College applications are one of the only opportunities outside of internships, programs, and future jobs that you are being evaluated as a candidate and as a person (approximately for both).

Before I get started, each person is evaluated hollistically in accordance to a rubric. If you didn't score high on one of the categories, you might be able to do well if you scored high on other categories. The following headings will entail general specifics(not actual) of what these requirements are, as some below are a mash of two sections of the rubric.

Grades & Academics:

Grades matter for the most part because they demonstrate your ability problem-solve and think at a high level. There is a higher emphasis on Sophomore and Junior year grades than Freshman and Senior grades since the latter few are used for fulfillment of A-G courses which I will explain later. Everybody starts fresh in their first year of high school, so if this sounds like you, take the opportunity to experiment with good habits that will be the foundation of your success in later years when you will take on heavier course load. UCs are particularly lenient(relatively to T20 universities) on grades because they take into account of the courses your school offers(Having a B+ Junior and Senior is no big deal, but make sure it's not a class related to what you are planning to major in if you are applying for a specific major). For example, if your school only offers mathematics up to AP Calculus BC, you would be considered as a top student in the context of the fact that you took the highest level of mathematics at your school compared to other people who haven't. If you took the same class during senior year but your school offers Linear Algebra or Diff Eq, then you would be considered less from top compared to students who did take the highest level of mathematics. This applies to other branches of study, though math is a good example of this. If you are at the edge of your school's cirriculum for a particular subject, you can consider doubling up on other subjects or take college "for-credit" classes. On the note for classes in senior year, make sure your classes in senior year are on-par with the classes you took the year before.

AP Testing is largely important to demonstrate your level of readiness for college classes. It's easy to think of them as actual colleges classes, but in reality the college classes are significantly more difficult than the actual AP exams.(Depending on skill level and subject of course) So it's more likely that the former case is true. UC Berkeley specifically has an entrance writing proficiency exam which students can use AP Lit/Lang score of higher than 3 to skip. Each AP class covers a lot of material, so again use them as opportunities to figure out the best study method in approaching these subjects

SAT is a nationalized standarized testing with sole purpose of evaluating how well you can analyze text. This is more of a benchmark than an exam that is used to evaluate the fullest extend of your ability to use english (namely if you've been reading at all since kindergarden). You could get away with getting a high score (1500+) through intensive training, but reading throughout high school will save you a lot of pain in the future.

\*All things mentioned above don't solely dictate you as a person. You can't expect to get into a good UC school with only straight A's, 5's on all APs, and 1600 SAT (From some people who I know)

What About Activities?

Activities themselves are important, but what's more important are (1) The reasoning behind your doing of these activities and (2) Why this is an important part of your identity. You obviously wouldn't be thinking about this consciously as you are doing them, but it's good to write reflections after each session of activities as they will be crucial resources for your future UC essays.
