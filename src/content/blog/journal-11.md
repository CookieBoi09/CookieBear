"Sometimes you just gotta larp your way in just to gain traction."

&emsp;I’ve recently looked back at my early years when I was just 7 years old, and I remember noticing a book called No Nonsense Algebra, which I have no idea how it was on the shelf in the first place. I saw the numbers and pluses and x’s which piqued my curiosity more in figuring out how to solve these problems. I spend hours just simply looking through the pages of math equations and started with the easier problems. I did end up giving up on the systems of equations problems which I had no clue at the time how it works, and I’ve pretty much never did algebra again until 4th grade when I began formally learning it in an online class. With this knowledge, I was able to do some AoPS problems(Art of Problem Solving books, which are a must-read-and-do for those interested in math competition) and pursue my interest in mathematics.

&emsp;Comparing this journey to my current state, I realized that I was able to understand mathematics not because I had the intellect to piece together concepts quickly but moreso because I had more exposure to the ideas which allowed my brain to slowly absorb the information. By the time I had to “formally” learn the ideas in my classes, I was able to pick up the concept quickly without having to spend a lot of time in the moment processing all the information. To be frank, this was the main advantage I had coming into middle and high school which didn’t work so well for my current journey in math in university. I breezed past the algebra and geometry classes which allowed me to take the year-long linear algebra class in my senior year of high school.

&emsp;The main point I want to get across is that a journey like this doesn’t happen overnight, and it starts with this common concept we’ve already seen in the internet called “larping”. Larping is generally perceived to have negative connotation, but with genuine curiosity and hard work done, larping could be a great catalyst for progress and improvement over time. When I say larping, I am referring to the state of trying to understand something when you probably don’t have the intuition to do so or trying to be someone who understands this particular topic. In either case, there is an effort made to understand the topic, and as a matter of fact it’s an important ingredient for gaining intuition in seeing mathematical concepts. Larping in trying to understand algebra at such a young age effectively built a level of thinking and intuition that supported me throughout my education in mathematics. Who knows if larping in a field of your interest can take you farther than you’ve ever imagined?

&emsp;(Note: You would also need to keep your skill level in mind and make reasonable decisions that can push you past your limit while growing at a healthy level)

&emsp;As I’m preparing for my midterms and continuing to work through my goals, I’m definitely going to be definitely larping ‘till I gain traction to being able to do the things I want to do in the future.
