&emsp;Right now it is 6:10 a.m. and no I have not slept yet. As a matter of fact I have not slept for over 24 hours (Which is a problem), but I’m actually participating in a hackathon right now at this moment. This hackathon is called CalHacks, one of the largest intercollegiate hackathons in the nation, where thousands of teams develop something to showcase to a series of judges. So far, this has been one of the best experiences I’ve ever had (Including drinking red bull for the first time though I didn’t have the original).

&emsp;To be honest, our group of 4 didn’t have a clue about what we were going to do until the literal night before when the hackathon started. It consists of my friend and two other undergraduate students from UIUC. We knew we were going to do something related to meta glasses since one of the people in our group have done a project on gaussian splatting (You can search it up on the internet if you want), but it’s not until the day of the hackathon when we got our hands on meta glasses and a VR headset(which my friend and I had no clue until then funny enough). We spent that day figuring out what idea we want to have and what kind of project we want to do for the hackathon. Through some discussion, we figured that we would focus on one of the tracks which called for a product that can being communities together. That being we use meta glasses to record 2D videos and train models that convert it to a 3D world where people can traverse through over time. The inspiration was admittedly my idea in which people discuss their cultures via language, which is relatively limited compared to images, which can show a lot more than just description of what it’s like to experience another culture. Especially in a world of busy people, some may not have as must leisure or flexibility to travel around the world, and having these experiences shared can bring people together in terms of being able to appreciate other cultures in ways beyond simply describing the details.

&emsp;We worked on it for hours and hours, making sure that the whole system is functional and that there is a working pipeline for the whole process. I personally worked on the back-end, making sure that it works in tangent with the web page, and the multiplayer aspect of the system, where a friend in a friend group and simultaneously share the “memories”(hence the name Memo) at the same time, which are all running in the same server. Admittedly, I did use AI for the project, but most of the backbone and the pipeline were our group’s original idea, and having the weight taken off of the execution of the idea actually strengthened our ability to create something that is more considerate for users, like trying to figure out the most efficient way for users to navigate between functionalities of our program. Overall, it was a great experience, though we have not been past the deadline (in two and a half hours) and judging, but we’ll see how we do. I know the level of product we have, and I know that there are strong competitors, but I know we will do great.

&emsp;Even if we didn’t get anything, this experience was one of the best learning opportunities in terms of working in a group, navigating conflicts, and thinking critically about a problem as close to as a start up would (except that the timeline of production is speed up very considerably.). Will have a second part, but I’m looking forward to presenting this project.
