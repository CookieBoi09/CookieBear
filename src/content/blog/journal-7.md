I remember back in middle school seeing many of the high schoolers’ projects in their respective groups and projects like ISS program or FRC(First Robotics Competition). I would think to myself that I might someday find a niche area which I can focus into, but little did I know I had no clue the whole entire time how I would do so. Fast forwarding to now as I am writing this page, I have an idea of the field that I am heading to which I have worked hours for throughout high school. But getting to this position took hours of thinking because of two reasons: I needed to figure out what kind of life I want to have in the future and I needed to find some inspiration which I can see myself getting immersed into.

Having lived in the bay area for some time now, it seems that getting a corporate job and making good amounts of money is the norm. Not to say that this kind of thinking is wrong, because every single person’s way of thinking is different and it’s completely acceptable to pursue a corporate job for a stable income and good life. Not that I wouldn’t like to work in a corporate job, it’s more so that I took more interest in doing research that can push the scientific frontier. A lot of content I have consumed from the media over the years were science-based: from bill-nye the science guy to Stephen hawking’s documentary to Startalk. Through their content, I became interested in the sciences at an early age, which propelled me to work hard in middle and high school just so that I can do interesting research in university years and beyond. I’m not interested in doing research for the sake of research but rather because of the interesting properties of observable objects (things we see) scientists have observed in the past.

Now that I've mentioned a little spiel of myself and how my interests came to be, I would like to revisit the two points I have mentioned earlier and how they can help you find your direction or “Lane” as the title suggest.

Before I go into the points, I would like to note that it is completely okay to not know what subject or field you want to go into. I personally didn’t know that I wanted to do applied physics in uni until the literal summer before uni started (I initially thought that I might do math or electrical engineering in the future). However, having some clarity in what you want to do in the future can be more beneficial to your chosen career.

Figuring out what kind of life you want to have in the future doesn’t entirely mean thinking about what specific job you want to work in. For me, I personally liked the start-up culture and having the flexible schedule to work in research at the same time. I wasn’t purely thinking “Oh I want to work in \*MANGOS or DataBricks”, but rather the type of environment I want to be. This will be important in finding your field, because the work culture varies between subject areas. You can love your field, but if the work culture is not what you wanted, then it’s very difficult to sustain the passion (Speaking from personal experience). Thinking about the life surrounding your general interest will propel you to a life you are willing to sacrifice your time and effort for.

Moreover, finding inspiration is easier said than done. You would need to spend effort to search for the materials or media that may interest you. In the case of college students, you will need to put effort into writing cold emails to post docs or professors you are interested in working with. Think of it like this: imagine you are in the ocean water near the beaches and you are trying to surf. Getting the timing right so that you will ride on the wave takes patience and effort, but once you get on that point, the rest of the journey is smooth, though you need to catch another one in order to surf again. Each wave is the media content you might find on the internet that may interest you, and you have to do work to find these content and continually enrich your journey pursuing a specific field. This is not the only way of finding a field that interests you, however, since some might come up like opportunities. At the end of the day, inspiration comes and goes through your life, but you have to put in the work if you want to continue pursue one. Apart from the effort required, if you are interested in looking for an interest, go explore on the internet! There are so many inspiring videos and content across various disciplines. Try to test the waters for one of them, and if it doesn’t work out, you can go look for another. As mentioned earlier, I was thinking of going into physics, but my interest shifted from theoretical to applied physics, which is totally acceptable because your interest may change over a long period of time.

With all this said, the bottom line is to just look around for anything that interests you can commit 110% to it until you want to redirect yourself into another.
